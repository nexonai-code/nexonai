GDPR and data protection

The system supports data protection. It does not make an organisation compliant: that depends on how it is used and operated. Here you see what the architecture does and what stays with you.

1

Who is who

The organisation that operates the servers and decides what is processed is the controller (Art. 4(7) GDPR).

NexonAI supplies software and licence codes, runs no servers and has no access to your data. On that basis NexonAI is normally not a processor (Art. 28).

If we gave support inside your operation with access to data, that would change and a contract would be needed. The hosting provider of a server is the customer’s processor. A lawyer makes the final classification.

3

How the architecture supports the principles

Data minimisation

Art. 5(1)(c)

No accounts, no phone numbers, no directory. The system does not collect identities.

Storage limitation

Art. 5(1)(e)

Packets on servers expire after 24 hours at most. Users can choose shorter periods.

Integrity and confidentiality

Art. 5(1)(f), Art. 32

Encryption from the phone, traffic through Tor, sealed server keys, no operator who can read the content.

Data protection by design

Art. 25

The principle of the whole system: there is no central place where personal data gathers.

4 · unpruuf Compliance

The whistleblower channel

EU Directive 2019/1937 requires that the identity of the whistleblower is not disclosed to anyone beyond the authorised staff handling reports (Art. 16).

Personal data is processed in line with the GDPR. Data clearly irrelevant to a report is not collected or is deleted without delay (Art. 17). Records of reports are kept no longer than necessary and proportionate (Art. 18).

unpruuf Compliance helps with the first point by construction: it captures no identity unless the whistleblower gives one. The other two stay with the organisation.

5

What stays with the organisation

The legal basis. For a channel required by law this is usually a legal obligation (Art. 6(1)(c)). The data protection officer confirms.
Informing data subjects, including people named in a report.
The record of processing activities (Art. 30) and considering whether an impact assessment is needed (Art. 35). Because reports can contain sensitive data, it is worth considering.
How long a case is kept and deleting what is no longer needed.
Notifying a personal data breach to the supervisory authority within 72 hours where required (Art. 33). In Romania that is ANSPDCP.
The contract with the hosting provider and the choice of where servers run.
Requests from data subjects.

The whitepaper describes the system and can be used for the organisation’s own documentation.

Honest limits

We do not claim GDPR compliance. There is no certification and no independent audit.

A compromised server that exposes its packets exposes encrypted packets without names. Whether and how that is assessed and reported is decided by the organisation’s data protection officer.

A function to delete or export a single case in the dashboard is not built yet.

Where servers run and the relays that carry the encrypted traffic raise questions about transfers. The organisation’s data protection officer assesses them.
